# Description 
In our study of religion over the last couple weeks we've been focusing on an early church heresy called Arianism which asserted (more or less) that Christ was not fully divine in the way that God is divine, but is rather a "first among equals" in terms of his position amongst the rest of creation. This heresy (I think) was what necessitated the Council of Nicea which produced the Nicene Creed. 

While listening to a [Catholic podcast](https://youtu.be/oH6_ZPiay9g?is=iEnnXWPxwibg2VAd) on the subject of Arianism by a bishop out of California, the presenter states that Arianism had to be rejected because Jesus
 1. needs to be fully divine in order to be able to grant us salvation/redemption 
 2. needs to be fully human so that his sacrifice applies to us
The way this ^ position is stated immediately sounded like pure cope to me, it sounded like self-fulfilling prophecy, or something contrived like that, which also brought to mind discussions I've heard in other videos/lectures about how the Gospels portray Jesus the way they do precisely to make him fit into existing Jewish prophesies known to people at the time. 

That said, I am mostly curious to understand exactly what Arius was asserting, and the Orthodox rebukes/rebuttals launched in response. This feels like something that can become a "Test Question" for Christians, highlighting how little they know about how the current presentation of Christianity came to be. 

Note also that Arianism was a heresy related to the earlier, similar heresies of Modalism/Sabellianism.
