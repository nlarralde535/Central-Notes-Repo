# Thesis:

Back in 2022 Florida Governor Ron Desantis signed into law Florida HB 1557, the Parental Rights in Education bill, know colloquially as the "Don't Say 'Gay' Bill".  I recall at the time bringing up this legislation in a conversation with Tio Eddy, probably in the context of government censorship sanctioned by the right. And I recall his response being "Show me where in the bill is says one can't say 'gay'", the point being that because the legislation did not explicitly prohibit Floridians to utter the word "gay" specifically, any/all criticism was rendered null. 

In the intervening years I have commented on how neither politicians nor people in general are able to be openly racist. Patrice O'Neil has a bit about this too. 

This essay means to put a fine point on this observation, using the particular example of the Jim Crow-era grandfather clause. This example is useful I think in highlighting the way nefarious intentions are intentionally buried in such a way that disingenuous people may plausibly claim ignorance when those intentions are pointed out. Also, the example of the grandfather clause is a law that no serious person today would defend. 

The objective of this essay is to understand the justifications/affirmative arguments for the implementation of the grandfather clause, and then compare those justifications to both the actual impacts on black votership, as well as to the character of modern-day justifications of things like the Don't Say Gay Bill.
