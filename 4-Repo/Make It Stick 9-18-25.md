Page xi
"The responsibility for learning rests with every individual." 
    ...I don't think I believe this. Individuals play a role (maybe even a primary role) in their own learning, but the responsibility to learn some shit cannot be said to rest exclusively with the individual. There'd be no need to compel children to go to school, or argue about what they should be taught, if that were truly so.

"Matt Brown was flying a twin-engine Cessna northeast out of Harlingen, Texas when he noticed a drop of oil pressure in his left engine. ... Matt has been messing around with piston engines since he was old enough to hold a wrench, and he knew he had a problem. ... his ability to work himself out of a jam illustrates what we mean in this book when we talk about learning: acquiring knowledge and skills and having them readily available from memory so you can make sense of future problems and opportunities."
    This ^ contradicts the previous quote, I think. Matt did not take responsibility for learning to work with piston engines from the time he could pick up a wrench, the people in his life at the time took responsibility to teach him and expose him. Yes, at some point he took that responsibility upon himself and furthered his knowledge/skills. But my point is proven: it takes a village, not a discrete set of individuals.
