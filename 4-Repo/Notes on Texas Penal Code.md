# Chapter 2 - Burden of Proof 

Sec. 2.01.  PROOF BEYOND A REASONABLE DOUBT. 
All persons are presumed to be innocent and no person may be convicted of an offense unless each element of the offense is proved beyond a reasonable doubt.  The fact that he has been arrested, confined, or indicted for, or otherwise charged with, the offense gives rise to no inference of guilt at his trial

# Chapter 9 - Justification Excluding Criminal Responsibility

[[Penal Code Title 2 Ch 9 - Justification Excluding Criminal Responsibility]]
 - "Deadly force" means force that is intended or known by the actor to cause, or in the manner of its use or intended use is capable of causing, death or serious bodily injury.
 - "The threat of force is justified when the use of force is justified by this chapter."
 - "The use of deadly force is not justified under this section unless the actor reasonably believes the deadly force is specifically required by statute or unless it occurs in the lawful conduct of war.  If deadly force is so justified, there is no duty to retreat before using it"
 - force is justified when used against "committing or attempting to commit aggravated kidnapping, murder, sexual assault, aggravated sexual assault, robbery, or aggravated robbery;"
 - force is not justified if you provoke a person
 - force is not justified as a response to verbal provocation alone
 - justification for resisting arrest 
 - force or deadly force can be used to protect a 3rd person 
 - a person who is not a peace officer is justified in using force to make an arrest

# Chapter 22 - Assaultive Offenses

Note: 
The following points seem to contradict the "guns don't kill people" nonsense 
 - assault becomes aggravated assault with the presence of a deadly weapon
 - deadly conduct goes from a Class A misdemeanor to a 3rd degree felony when a firearm is discharged 

[[Penal Code Title 5 Ch 22 - Assaultive Offenses]]
 - threatening someone counts as assault: "intentionally or knowingly threatens another with imminent bodily injury" 
 - assault is a Class A misdemeanor 
     - 3rd degree felony if committed against a public servant
     - 2nd degree felony if committed against a peace officer 
 - the difference between assault and aggravated assault is 1. degree of bodily injury inflicted (bodily injury vs. "serious" bodily injury) and 2. presence of a deadly weapon (whether used in the assault or merely exhibited)
     - minimum 2nd degree felony
 - necessary mental states for assault: "intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly"
 - deadly conduct: "recklessly engages in conduct that places another in imminent danger of serious bodily injury."
     -  Class A misdemeanor 
 - deadly conduct: "knowingly discharges a firearm at or in the direction of ... one or more individuals or a habitation"
     - 3rd degree felony
 - terroristic threat is characterized by " threats to commit any offense involving violence to any person or property with intent to..." followed by 6 different things. 
     - punishments range from Class B misdemeanor to state jail felony

# Chapter 38 - Obstructing governmental operation

Questions: 
 - what is a "lawful arrest"?

[[Penal Code Title 8 - Chapter 38 Obstructing governmental operation]]
 - failure to identify
     - Class C to Class B misdemeanor 
 - resisting arrest or search: "intentionally prevents or obstructs a person he knows is a peace officer ... from effecting an arrest, search, or transportation of the actor or another by using force against the peace officer "
     - ! ! ! It is no defense to prosecution under this section that the arrest or search was unlawful.
     - Class A misdemeanor 
     - 3rd degree felony if the actor uses a deadly weapon
 - taking weapon from peace officer: "It is a defense to prosecution under this section that the defendant took or attempted to take the weapon from a peace officer ... who was using force against the defendant or another in excess of the amount of force permitted by law"
 - interference with public duties: "interrupts, disrupts, impedes, or otherwise interferes with a peace officer"
     - It is a defense to prosecution under this section that the interruption, disruption, impediment, or interference alleged consisted of speech only.

# Chapter 46 - Weapons

[[Penal Code Title 10 - Chapter 46 Weapons]]
 - definitions for terms such as club, firearm and location-restricted knife
 - without a license you're allowed to carry a handgun 1. in your own premises or 2. in or directly en route to a vehicle you own
 - unlawful carrying a weapon: "A person commits an offense if the person carries a handgun and intentionally displays the handgun in plain view of another person in a public place"
     - "It is an exception to the application of this subsection that the handgun was partially or wholly visible but was carried in a holster"
     - Class A misdemeanor if the weapon is a handgun
     - Class C misdemeanor if the weapon is a location-restricted knife 
 - places weapons are prohibited: on the premises of a school or postsecondary educational institution, polling place on election day, courts, hospitals
     - punishments range from 3rd degree felony to Class C misdemeanor 
     - license holders may concealed-carry on school campuses
     - license holders commit an offense when they "intentionally or knowingly display the handgun in plain view of another person"

# Chapter 76 Terroristic Offenses

Sec. 76.02.  TERRORISM.
(a)  A person commits an offense if the person:
    (1)  commits or conspires to commit an offense under:
        (A)  Chapter 19;
        (B)  Section 20.02;
        (C)  Section 20.03;
        (D)  Section 20A.02;
        (E)  Section 22.02;
        (F)  Section 22.021;
        (G)  Section 22.05, if punishable as a felony;
        (H)  Section 22.07, if punishable as a felony;
        (I)  Section 22.09;
        (J)  Section 28.02;
        (K)  Section 28.07, if punishable as a felony;
        (L)  Section 29.03;
        (M)  Section 38.152, if punishable as a felony; or
        (N)  Section 46.08; and
    (2)  commits or conspires to commit that offense with the intent to:
        (A)  intimidate or coerce the public or a substantial group of the public; or
        (B)  influence, by intimidation or coercion, the policy, conduct, or activities of this state, a political subdivision of this state, or the United States
