Low-stakes or no-stakes testing promotes retrieval of newly leaned information. The point of testing is not to "gauge learning" but to reinforce learning by inducing retrieval. The focus of the testee should be on retrieval, not on the consequences of potentially failing the test. 

Page 39
Delaying feedback on incorrect test responses is more effective at strengthening retention than providing immediate feedback. Instead allow the testee to think about why the incorrect responses might be incorrect. 

Page 41
"While any kind of retrieval practice benefits learning, the implication seems to be that where more cognitive effort is required for retrieval, greater retention results. ... the act of retrieving a memory changes the memory, making it easier to retrieve again later"

Page 44
Self-testing can take the form of ***flash cards***, both creating the flashcards and then using them to test oneself are solid learning exercises.

"The rapid gains from \[ focused, repetitive\] massed practice are often evident, but the rapid forgetting that follows is not. ... \[ The benefits of spaced, interleaved, and varied practice\] come at a price: when practice is spaced, interleaved, and varied, it requires more effort.
