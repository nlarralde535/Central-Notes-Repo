## On the psychology related to habit formation

Page 45 [[Atomic Habits 9-10-25]]
"Whenever you face a problem repeatedly, your brain begins to automate the process of solving it. ... 'Habits are simply reliable solutions to recurring problems in our environment.' ... As habits are created, the level of activity in the brain decreases."

Page 46 [[Atomic Habits 9-10-25]]
"Habit formation is incredibly useful because the conscious mind is the bottleneck of the brain. ... Habits reduce cognitive load and free up mental capacity so you can allocate your attention to other tasks."

Page 49 - 50 [[Atomic Habits 9-11-25]]
"If a behavior is insufficient in any of \[ the four stages of building a habit (Cue - Craving - Response - Reward) \] it will not become a habit. ... Without the first three stages, the behavior will not occur. Without all four, the behavior will not be repeated. " 

Page 54 - 55:
 - A habit is a behavior that has been repeated enough times to become automatic
 - Any habit can be broken down into a feedback loop that involves four steps: 
     - cue
     - craving
     - response 
     - reward
 The Four Laws of Behavior change correspond to these ^ feedback loop steps
1. Make it Obvious
2. Make it Attractive
3. Make it Easy
4. Make it Satisfying
where '*it*' refers to the behavior your are trying to turn into a habit.  

Page 73 [[Atomic Habits 9-11-25]]
"No behavior happens in isolation. Each action becomes a cue that triggers the next behavior"

Page 111 [[Atomic Habits 9-12-25]]
"It is the anticipation of a reward - not the fulfillment of it - that gets us to take action. The greater the anticipation, the greater the dopamine spike"

Page 160 - 161 [[Atomic Habits 9-16-25]]
"Habits are like the entrance ramp to a highway. They lead you down a path and before you know it, you're speeding towards the next behavior. ... We are limited by where our habits lead us. ... Habits are the entry point, not the end point."

Page 191 [[Atomic Habits 9-17-25]]
"What we're really talking about here is the in ending of s behavior ... because we tend to remember it more than other phases. You want the ending of your habit to be satisfying. The best approach is to use ***reinforcement*** , which tied your habit to an immediate reward."

Page 201 [[Atomic Habits 9-17-25]]
On recovering when your habits break down: 
"Too often we fall into an all-or-nothing cycle with our habits. The problem is not slipping up (this is inevitable); the problem is thinking that it you can't do something perfectly then you shouldn't do it at all. You don't realize how valuable it is (to the formation of habits) to just show up on your bad days.

## Practical Tips for building new habits

Page 63 [[Atomic Habits 9-11-25]]
***Pointing-and-calling***: a safety system used by Japanese public railway workers is used to make workers more conscious of the details that make up their jobs in order to more easily identify when something is not right and thus prevent accidents. 
"... it raises the level of awareness form a non conscious habit to a more conscious level"

From page 62: "Before we can effectively build new habits, we need to get a handle on our current ones. This can be more challenging that it sounds because once a habit is firmly rooted in your life it is non-conscious and automatic."
From page 63: "As each operator runs the train, they proceed through a ritual of pointing out different objects and calling out specific commands. ... Every detail is identified, pointed at, and named aloud."

This is where ***pointing-and-calling*** comes in to play: you can go through your day, or parts of your day, and point-and-call the things you are doing in order to give conscious attention to each action/activity that until now you had been doing unconsciously. From that point, for each behavior you identify through pointing-and-calling, you can ask the question "Do I want to continue this behavior?". 

---

Page 70 [[Atomic Habits 9-11-25]]
***Implementation Intentions*** are explicit plans one makes before engaging in an act/behavior, which detail WHEN and WHERE the act/behavior will be done. "Implementation intentions leverage the cues of time and location, the two most common cues that trigger habits"
We did this ^ by linking brushing our teeth with leaving the house. 

Implementation intentions are explicit, specific plans for how you intend to implement a habit/behavior. 
You can use these to plan ahead the behaviors/habits you wish to form: 
    *When situation X arises, I will perform behavior Y*
or
    *I will \[BEHAVIOR\] at \[TIME\] in \[LOCATION\]*

From page 71: "Many people think they lack motivation, when what they really lack is clarity. ... Once an implementation intention is set, you don't have to wait for inspiration to strike."
From page 72: "The goal is to make the time and location so obvious that, with enough repetition, you get an urge to do the right thing at the right time."

---

Page 74 [[Atomic Habits 9-11-25]]
***Habit Stacking*** is a special firm of an ***Implementation Intentions*** where you attach a desired new habit to an exiting habit, instead of simply to some predetermined time and location. 
"After \[CURRENT HABIT\] I will do \[NEW HABIT\]"

Page 78 [[Atomic Habits 9-11-25]]
"Habit Stacking works best when the cue is highly specific and immediately actionable. ... Habits like 'read more' and 'eat better' are worthy causes, but these goals do not provide sufficiently specific instructions on how and when to act."

You can "stack" new habits on top of existing habits. 

---

Page 109 - 110 [[Atomic Habits 9-12-25]]
***Temptation Bundling*** is when you "bundle" and activity that you want/are inclined to do with another activity that you should/have to do. 
"Temptation Bundling is a way of applying a psychology theory called Premack's Principle, which states that 'more probable behaviors will reinforce less probable behaviors'. 

From page 109: "You're more like to find a behavior attractive if you get to do one of your favorite things at the same time."

Here you can "bundle" and activity that you are already tempted to do with a new behavior you are trying to get in the habit of doing. 
    *While I"m doing X, which I already enjoy doing, I will also do Y, which is something I need to start doing regularly

Or you can place a new behavior "in the way" of your already-established temptation activity
    *Only after I do \[NEW BEHAVIOR\] am I allowed to do \[BEHAVIOR I ENJOY\]*

***Habit stacking*** and ***temptation bundling*** can be used together to create a structure for deciding what to do and when to do it. 

---

Page 163 [[Atomic Habits 9-16-25]]
***The Two Minute Rule*** "the idea is to make your habits as easy as possible to start \[ by scaling the corresponding behavior down into a 2-minute version that is exceedingly easy to do\]. ... this is a powerful strategy because once you've started doing the right thing, it is much easier to continue doing it. ... The point \[at the beginning\] is to master the habit of showing up. The truth is, a habit must be established before it can be improved."

From page 162: "Even when you know you should start small, it's easy to start too big ... \[An\] effective way to counteract this tendency is to use the ***Two-Minute Rule*** which states '*When you start a new habit, it should take less than 2 minutes to do*'".

The hard part here ^ might be figuring out how to scale **down** the habit you want to start doing (example: running 3x per week) to something that only takes 2 minutes to do, but is still directly related to the original habit. Some trial and error might be required. 

---

Page 170 [[Atomic Habits 9-16-25]]
***commitment device*** is a choice you make in the present that controls your actions in the future

From page 169: "Sometimes success is less about making good habits **easy** to do and more about making bad habits **hard** to do. "
From page 171: "***Commitment devices*** increase the odds that you'll do the right thing in the future by making bad habits difficult in the present."

Basically, these are decisions you make in the present that "lock you in" to a particular behavior, as much as possible. One example is buying a bunch of groceries at the start of the week and then making a bunch of individually prepped meals: this will "lock you in" to eating at home (desired behavior) instead of eating out (undesired behavior). 

## Practical Tips for eliminating existing habits

Page 55:
Invert the Four Laws of Behavior Change to get a set of rules for breaking bad habits: 
1. Make it invisible
2. Make it unattractive
3. Make it difficult
4. Make it unsatisfying

---

Page 155 [[Atomic Habits 9-16-25]]
"Much of the battle of building better habits comes down to finding ways to reduce the friction associated with our good habits and increase the friction associated with our bad ones."

From page 153: "Habits are easier to build when they fit into the flow of your life"
From page 155: "The central idea is to create an environment where doing the right thing is as easy as possible."

Once you are aware of all of your habits (good and bad) from ***Pointing-and-calling***, you can rearrange your environment to make the behaviors you want to encourage easier to perform, and (maybe more importantly) make the behaviors you want to discourage harder to perform. 

---

Page 158 [[Atomic Habits 9-16-25]]
"It is remarkable how little friction is required to prevent unwanted behavior."

From page 156: "Whenever you organize a space for its intended purpose, you are priming it to make the next action easy."
From page 157: "You can also invert this principle and prime the environment to make the bad behaviors difficult."

## On not swimming against the tide of human nature

Page 32: [[Atomic Habits 9-9-25]]
"Behind every system of actions there is a system of beliefs. ... A similar pattern exists when we are discussing individuals, organizations, or societies. ... \[there is an identity behind the habits.\] Behavior that is incongruent with the self will not last"

From page 33: "It's hard to change your habits if you never change the underlying beliefs that lead to your past behavior."

---

Page 40: [[Atomic Habits 9-9-25]]
"The concept of identity-based habits is our first \[ intro to feedback loops \]. "
Habits shape identity and visa versa: it is important to let your identity (or the identity you're trying to inhabit), and NOT your day-to-day results, drive the loop. 

From page 39: Simple two-step process for changing your identity: 
1. decide the type of person you want to be and 
2. prove it to yourself with small wins

From page 40: "Once you have a handle of the type of person you want to be, you can begin taking small steps to reinforce your desired identity."

"...there are many different ways to address the same underlying motive. ... Your current habits are not necessarily the best ways to solve the problems you face. ... Life feels reactive, but it is actually predictive. ... Our behavior is heavily dependent on these predictions. Put another way, our behavior is heavily dependent on how we interpret the events that happen to us, not necessarily on the objective reality of the events themselves.

Page 142 - 143 [[Atomic Habits 9-15-25]]
"As Voltaire once wrote: the best is the enemy of the good. I refer to this as the difference between being in motion and taking action. ... When you're 'in motion' you're planning and strategizing and learning. Those are all good things, but they don't produce a result. Action, on the other hand, is the type of behavior that will deliver an outcome. ... You don't want to merely be planning. You want to be practicing."
This ^ is where we have been stuck for a long while: being in motion.

Page 190 [[Atomic Habits 9-17-25]]
"Thankfully it is possible to train yourself to delay gratification - but you need to work with the grain of human nature, not against it. The best way to do this is to add a little bit of immediate pleasure to the habits that pay off in the long run and a little bit of immediate pain to ones that don't"
