(8) Unemoyment is inevitable. Specialization and division of labor makes it that much more precarious for shifts in demand to displace specialized/skilled workers, as opposed to unskilled workers. And we know how volatile is the market for unskilled labor. 

It costs money/time to retrain oneself for a new line of work. 

Suppose we had a system that extended assistance to out-of-work individuals looking to retrain, and that once ready to enter the workforce they were palced into a pool of labor from which folks are drawn and place into contact with businesses looking for their particular skills. And the individual is not taken off of the assistance benefit until they have been hired. 

This takes much of the hiring/recruiting costs out of the hands of businesses, and it limits the duration of time one spends collecting benefits to precisely that period if time where one is unemployed.
