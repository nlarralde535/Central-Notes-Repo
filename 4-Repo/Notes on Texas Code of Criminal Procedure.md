# Description 
The following note contains portions of noteworthy articles from those chapters of the [Texas Code of Criminal Procedure](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=CR&chapter=CR.1&artSec=) which we deemed worth noting for ease of future reference. 

# Chapter 1

**Art. 1.03. OBJECTS OF THIS CODE.** 
This Code is intended to embrace rules applicable to the prevention and prosecution of offenses against the laws of this State, and to make the rules of procedure in respect to the prevention and punishment of offenses intelligible to the officers who are to act under them, and to all persons whose rights are to be affected by them

**Art. 1.16. LIBERTY OF SPEECH AND PRESS.**
Every person shall be at liberty to speak, write or publish his opinion on any subject, being liable for the abuse of that privilege; 

**Art. 1.24. PUBLIC TRIAL.**
The proceedings and trials in all courts shall be public

# Chapter 2A - Officers Powers and Duties
[[Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 2A - Officers Powers and Duties]]
 - there are 35 different types of "peace officer"
 - ICE agents are not peace officers, but "special investigators"
 - residents may be summoned by a peace officer to "overcome resistance" met by an officer
     - residents are required to obey when summoned
 - failure on the part of a peace officer to execute "any legal process" is a civil offense with max fine of $200
 - peace officers are allowed to ask crime victims for their immigration status 
 - peace officers do not have to honor immigration detainers if the individual provides evidence of citizenship (Texas driver's license)
 - by March of each year the Office of the Attorney General must publish a public report detailing "Officer-involved injuries or deaths" as well as officer deaths (https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/open-government/open-reports-and-publications)

# Chapter 2B - Law Enforcement Interactions with Public
[[Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 2B - Law Enforcement Interactions with Public]]
 - The Texas Commission on Law Enforcement ("Commission") is introduced
 - law enforcement agencies must establish a racial profiling policy
 - law enforcement agencies must collect information about motor vehicle stops including racial information, and submit an annual report to TCOLE (https://tcole.texas.gov/content/racial-profiling-reports)
 - law enforcement agencies that receive state grants for body cameras must adopt a policy for use of those cameras, including when they must be turned on and when they can be turned off
 - peace officers who turn off or fail to turn on their body cameras when responding to a call for assistance must justify having done so
 - "A peace officer has a duty to intervene to stop or prevent another peace officer from using force against a person suspected of committing an offense..."

# Chapter 14 - Arrest without warrant

**Art. 14.01. OFFENSE WITHIN VIEW.**  
(a)  A peace officer **OR ANY OTHER PERSON**, may, without a warrant, arrest an offender when the offense is committed in his presence or within his view, if the offense is one classed as a felony or as an offense against the public peace.

(b) A peace officer may arrest an offender without a warrant for any offense committed in his presence or within his view

**Art. 14.03. AUTHORITY OF PEACE OFFICERS.** 
(a)  Any peace officer may arrest, without warrant:

    (6)  a person who makes a statement to the peace officer that would be admissible against the person under Article 38.21 and establishes probable cause to believe that the person has committed a felony

# Chapter 15 - Arrest under warrant
[[Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 15 - Arrest under warrant]]
 - a warrant is "a written order from a magistrate, directed to a peace officer or some other person specially named, commanding him to take the body of the person accused of an offense, to be dealt with according to law"
 - a warrant must have the following 
     - a name or description of a particular individual
     - the law(s) the named individual is found to have broken
     - the signature and office of a magistrate
 - a magistrate may issue a summons, which is similar to a warrant "except that it shall summon the defendant to appear before a magistrate at a stated time and place."
 - a "complaint" is an affidavit made before a magistrate which "charges the commission of an offense"
 - "arrest" is defined as when one is "placed under restraint or taken into custody by an officer or person executing a warrant of arrest"
     - "In making an arrest, all reasonable means are permitted to be used to effect it.  No greater force, however, shall be resorted to than is necessary to secure the arrest and detention of the accused"
 - "The officer need not have the warrant in his possession at the time of the arrest, provided the warrant was issued under the provisions of this Code, but upon request he shall show the warrant to the defendant as soon as possible"

# Chapter 19A - Grand jury organization

Questions
 - district judges are responsible for impaneling grand juries. Where are the "districts" defined?
     - note: the term "judicial district" appears in 19A.201.a-1
Notes
 - the sections on Grand jury qualifications and challenges to grand juror selection inform the point we often make when discussing why it is ridiculous to allow nation states to adjudicate crimes they allege have been committed against them: "you're not allowed to sit on a jury for a crime committed against yourself"

[[Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 19A - Grand jury organization]]
 - "The judge shall test the qualifications for and exemptions from service as a grand juror and impanel the completed grand jury"
 - Grand juror qualification point: is not a complainant in any matter to be heard by the grand jury during the term of court for which the person has been selected as a grand juror
 - reasons for which selection of particular Grand jurors may be challenged:
     - the grand juror has a bias or prejudice in favor of or against the person accused or suspected of committing an offense that the grand jury is investigating
     - that from hearsay, or otherwise, there is established in the mind of the grand juror a conclusion as to the guilt or innocence of the person accused or suspected of committing an offense that the grand jury is investigating 
 - part of the oath of Grand jurors: "...You shall present no person from envy, hatred or malice;  neither shall you leave any person unpresented for love, fear, favor, affection or hope of reward; but you shall present things truly as they come to your knowledge,"

# Chapter 21 - Indictment and Information

Questions
 - Art 21.14 refers to perjury and aggravated perjury, but these are nowhere defined in this chapter. Where in the Codes is perjury defined?

Notes
 - Art 21.16 makes clear why "you can get a grand jury to indict a ham sandwich"
 - Indictments are issued by grand juries, while informations are issued by courts themselves (by district/county attorneys)

[[Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 21 - Indictment and Information]]
 - An "indictment" is the written statement of a grand jury accusing a person therein named of some act or omission which, by law, is declared to be an offense
     - It shall be signed officially by the foreman of the grand jury
 - An indictment shall be deemed sufficient which charges the commission of the offense in ordinary and concise language in such a manner as to enable a person of common understanding to know what is meant
 - An "Information" is a written statement filed and presented in behalf of the State by the district or county attorney, charging the defendant with an offense which may by law be so prosecuted.
     - It must be signed by the district or county attorney, officially
