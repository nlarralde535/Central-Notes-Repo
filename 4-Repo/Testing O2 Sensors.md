### General Notes:
[This video](https://youtu.be/DvKZ2GDYEz4?si=jrY0zTpryfaA00sI) shows how to test continuity of the heater circuit in the sensor, and [this link](https://www.justanswer.co.uk/honda/nn03z-fuse-oxygen-sensor-honda-crv-2004.html) says the Ohms reading should be 3.3Ohms

Videos that cover testing voltage + continuity
[Voltage + heater circuit resistance](https://youtu.be/iVjJwJCt2Hg?si=4jMq68DDIcw-ABAZ)
[Heater circuit resistance](https://youtu.be/CviauEqXTLk?si=8EkfQ11wgKR3BDQO) 
[Article with various test methods](https://www.google.com/amp/s/www.carparts.com/blog/how-to-test-an-o2-sensor/amp/).

Also, take the sensor out and give it a shake: if you hear stuff rattling around inside then the sensor is broken/bad

[This link](https://www.reddit.com/r/MechanicAdvice/comments/l74901/o2_sensor_high_voltage/) states that high voltage could be due to bad heater circuit ground.

### CR-V specific: 

Check the No. 6 fuse (7.5A) in the under dash fide box. 

Our readings: HO2 sensor 2
 - ~5V (read from the harness)
 - ~7.2Ohms heater circuit (read from sensor)
