Hackworth describes Colonel Glover Johns being reassigned from his post in West Berlin. The following are points that Hackworth notes from Johns' farewell address to his troops: 
 - teach and lead by example
 - strive to do small things well 
 - be a "do-er" and a self-starter.
 - Aggressiveness and initiative are important, but so is stopping and thinking before you act
 - strive for self improvement through constant self-realization and never be satisfied 
 - ask of any project "how can it be done better?"
 - don't over-inspect or over-supervise; allow your people room to make mistakes and learn from them
 - keep your people informed, telling them What, How, and Why to maintain their confidence 
 - learn to speak and write well
 - have consideration for others

# Chapter 6 The only game in town
Duration 1:40:07

50:00
Hackworth contrasts the manner in which the "rear echelon" "stateside" army leaders interfered with  team and platoon cohesion, due to their perception of team commanderie as "signs of a loose, sloppy, undisciplined outfit" lacking order and unit effectiveness, with Colonel Sloan (Slone?) leaving management of the Wolfhound Raiders and the execution of mission orders completely to Lt. Hackworth himself. Hackworth tells how Barney K Neal's career as a platoon leader was a casualty of this meddling. This reminds me alot of the issues surrounding working  on a SCRUM team, quote: 
    "Fortunately we Riaders had a patron saint in Colonel Sloan...we had no visitors from higher headquarters, no staff inspections, no checks to see if we were following regulations. We'd get a mission order that said 'do it' and we trained and planned we saw fit. We wrote the book for the sorts of things we were doing instead of blindly following field manuals that didn't always apply. Sloan trusted me, so I had total freedom to get the job done. I trusted my NCOs to help me do it. My guys trusted me to stand up and fight if someone  tried to screw us over. And I trusted Sloan not to use us as a kamikaze force" 

23:19
The Wolfhound Raiders while in a recon mission anticipate a Chinese ambush by detecting the enemies scent. "Curiously, the intelligence value of the enemy's smell only had intelligence value to the troops on the ground." Phil Gilchrist told Hackworth a story about "rear-echelon commandos" in a division-level TOC: Hackworth radioed in a report to Gilchrist of approaching because the Wolfhound Raiders could smell them, the "boys at division though this was funny and asked how many of the enemy Hackworth could smell". Gilchrist was the only one in the TOC who had ever smelled the enemy, but kept his mouth shut because "the last thing a combat officer can do is intrude upon the ruminations of the non-fighting elite of the army". 

Hackworth goes on to conclude: "Was it any wonder the  that every fighting man in Korea was sent into the field smelling like the corner drug store?"
