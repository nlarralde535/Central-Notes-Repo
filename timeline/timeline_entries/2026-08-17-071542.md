# Meidas Touch News
According to the account provided by his attorney, the incident began on the morning of August 11, at approximately 6:30 a.m., as Jose Mejia Hernandez, was walking to work. A truck rapidly approached him, and two men got out and ran toward him. Frightened and unsure who they were or what they wanted, he ran. While fleeing, he says he was struck with what he believes was a stun gun or Taser. He has no memory of what happened next, including how he sustained his injuries, how he was transported, or how he ended up at a hospital. He says he next recalls waking up many hours later.

When he regained consciousness, Mejia Hernandez discovered significant facial injuries consistent with blunt force trauma that he has no memory of sustaining. Throughout his hospitalization, his attorney says two ICE agents remained at his bedside at all times, and he was never given a private opportunity to speak with his medical providers.

When he asked the agents what had happened to him, they told him they did not know. His attempts to get information from hospital staff, according to his lawyer, were met with limited answers, and staff appeared reluctant to speak openly with the agents present. He says he came to believe that medical staff were communicating more with the ICE officers than with him, leaving him largely in the dark about his own condition.

When ICE officers determined he was to be discharged, his attorney says Mejia Hernandez himself did not understand he was actually being released. He was transported from the hospital, offered food and drink, and told to contact his family. From there he was taken to an ICE office, fitted with a GPS ankle monitor, and issued immigration paperwork, including a Notice to Appear, with instructions to report to ISAP the following Monday. At the time of his discharge, he still did not understand the full extent of his injuries or what had happened to him before he woke up in the hospital.


According to his attorney, significant questions remain unanswered. Mejia Hernandez does not know what happened between the moment he was incapacitated and when he regained consciousness. He does not know how his injuries were sustained or what force, if any, was used against him. His family was not informed of his whereabouts for roughly three days. Throughout his hospitalization, ICE agents remained continuously at his bedside, which he believes limited his ability to speak privately with medical staff, and he believes hospital personnel communicated more with the agents than with him directly. He was discharged without a full explanation of his condition, only to require readmission for the same injuries days later, and he continues to face obstacles obtaining diagnostic imaging because of the monitoring requirements imposed on him.
