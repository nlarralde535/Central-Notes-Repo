# Snopes
The Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office said Miller was found dead early June 18, 2026, and that her death was "consistent with a suicide."

In June 2026, a claim (archived) circulated online that Tonea Miller, a 27-year-old Black woman, was found lynched from a tree after traveling to Miami, to celebrate Juneteenth, the annual June 19 holiday that commemorates the emancipation of enslaved African Americans.

The Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office said in a statement (archived) on June 24 that Miller's body was discovered early on June 18. An investigation by the Sheriff's Office and the county medical examiner found no evidence of foul play but rather that the circumstances of Miller's death were "consistent with a suicide."

Following that statement, some social media users questioned the authorities' findings. One X user wrote, "The Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office initially classified the incident as an 'apparent suicide' but no one is buying that at all."

Similar claims that said Miller had been lynched circulated on Facebook (archived), Instagram (archived) and Bluesky (archived). Snopes readers contacted us, asking about the circumstances around Miller's death.

According to the Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office, Miller appeared to have died by suicide. Major Vernon L. Williams repeated that Miller's death was not a result of foul play during a June 26 news conference (at 10:05). The Sheriff's Office did not say that Miller was found hanging from a tree, as some posts said. Tania Francois, a reporter for Miami CBS affiliate WFOR, reported that Miller was found in Gwen Cherry Park.

During the June 26 news conference, Terri Miller, Miller's sister, staunchly rejected the conclusion that Miller's death was a suicide. Terri Miller said (at 15:51), "My sister was a beautiful human that loved life and would not damn sure take her own." Miller also addressed "crap" on social media, denying claims that her sister had traveled to Miami for Juneteenth or that her death was necessarily tied to race.8

# Miami Times
The sister of To'Nea Nicole Miller publicly rejected the Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office's conclusion that the 27-year-old's death was consistent with suicide during a June 26 briefing at Gwen Cherry Park, where officials said investigators found no evidence of foul play.

In a statement released last Wednesday, the Miami-Dade Sheriff’s Office said the death was “consistent with suicide.” Major Vernon Williams repeated that claim at Friday’s briefing.

“After Miss Miller was discovered on Thursday, June 18, 2026, at approximately 6:20 a.m., deputies and detectives immediately began an extensive investigation,” said Williams. “During this investigation, detectives found no evidence that Miss Miller's death was a result of foul play.”

While Teri Miller rejected the Sheriff’s Office’s conclusion, NBC6 reported that To’Nea Miller's aunt, Michele Howell, had a different reaction.

"After talking with the investigator on the case and speaking with the medical examiner ... it was suicide," Howell told NBC6 on Friday. "...After gathering information and investigating, it was not homicide." 

Williams said investigators had closed the case as of June 26.
