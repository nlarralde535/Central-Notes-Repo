# Middle East Eye
Yemen's Houthi fighters' rapid advance along the Red Sea coast has put them in a prime position to control shipping in the Bab el-Mandeb choke point, while bolstering Iran’s leverage against the US, experts tell Middle East Eye

After days of heavy fighting along a narrow plain known as the Tihama, on Thursday the Houthis routed Yemeni forces loyal to Saudi Arabia and seized the historic and strategic Red Sea port city of Mocha.

Arab media reported that the Houthis made further gains throughout the day. They deployed troops on the Hanish Islands, which sit in the middle of the Red Sea across from Eritrea. Meanwhile, Houthi forces continued to advance south to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the island of Perim.

The Houthis' rapid advance has also raised questions about the coordination of Saudi-backed forces in Yemen.

Despite advancing down an open plain, the Houthis did not face significant air power from Saudi Arabia and its Yemeni allies.

Mocha was held by the National Resistance Forces (NRF), a well-equipped anti-Houthi force led by Tarek Saleh, the nephew of former Yemeni President Ali Abdullah Saleh, who was killed by the Houthis after allying with them a decade ago.

Saleh’s force was traditionally backed by the UAE, but shifted its allegiance to Saudi Arabia earlier this year. 

The UAE and Saudi Arabia led a military coalition to oust the Houthis a decade ago, but have since fallen out over the war in Sudan, Israel and Yemeni separatists.

The UAE announced its withdrawal from Yemen earlier this year, with Saudi Arabia assuming responsibility towards military groups aligned with Abu Dhabi. 

One western diplomat told MEE that the offensive came against the backdrop of talks between Saudi Arabia and the UAE about the latter potentially returning to Yemen to cover costs. MEE was told the talks were at an impasse.
