# Dropsite News
Alexandra Bravo, the prosecutor assigned to investigate U.S. strikes on three Ecuadorian boats and the 36 survivors’ subsequent accounts of torture and kidnapping, was shot dead in the coastal city of Manta last month. According to police, on June 14 a hitman aboard a black motorcycle opened fire on Bravo as she and her sister were leaving a café at about 11 a.m. Bravo’s sister, who jumped to protect her, was also killed in the attack.

Upon arriving at the crime scene, officers say they found five 9mm shell casings and Bravo’s injured driver, but no sign of the police detail that had been assigned to protect her. The Police Directorate, which operates entirely under the executive branch, has launched a probe to determine if there’d been an internal security breach.

Under Ecuador’s right-wing president Daniel Noboa—a Miami-born billionaire often characterized as a “U.S.-backed dictator”—the country has become a central testing ground for Operation Southern Spear, Washington’s extrajudicial bombing campaign purporting to target “narco-terrorists,” which has killed 221 people to date. There is no evidence that many of the victims, including those in Bravo’s investigation, had engaged in drug trafficking.

A source inside the Manta police department, who knew Bravo personally, told Drop Site that the Ecuadorian prosecutor was under pressure from her superiors at the Attorney General’s office to treat the U.S. attacks as exclusively “cases of disappearance.” The source, who asked for anonymity out of fear of retaliation, said Bravo was told to close all lines of inquiry after 36 of the 44 missing men were found—despite arriving with severe injuries, including 70% vision loss and feet torn to flesh and bone, which medical experts found consistent with their accounts of explosions and torture.

A senior researcher with Human Rights Watch who interviewed Bravo as part of her investigation into the three vessels, similarly recalled Bravo facing “internal challenges within the institution itself to advance the investigation.” 

In the days leading up to her death, Bravo was relocated to the Attorney General’s office in Montecristi, a neighboring municipality. Her caseload changed after the transfer and she was no longer in charge of investigating the three boat attacks. Drop Site attempted to reach Bravo’s replacement for comment but he had been explicitly instructed not to speak to the press. After weeks of attempts to reach the Attorney General’s office for comment, they told Drop Site the investigation was sealed and they could not comment on why Bravo was relocated.

Inside Ecuador’s U.S.-backed military regime, the Attorney General’s office has become an increasingly opaque and politicized institution. “It’s already a major obstacle for prosecutors to investigate local state agents. Now imagine international state agents,” Bastias said. “The Executive Branch has implemented a series of legal frameworks that leave these foreign state agents immune from prosecution,” he added, referring to the diplomatic immunity granted to all U.S. military and Department of Defense personnel through President Noboa’s 22 martial law decrees.
