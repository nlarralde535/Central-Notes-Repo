# DC News Now
Police and the county executive said Monday that a man whom someone found hanging from a tree Sunday died by suicide.

Members of the Prince George’s County Police Department (PGPD) went to the 9300 block of D’Arcy Road in the unincorporated section of Upper Marlboro around 7:55 a.m. where they found the man hanged.

Detectives began a death investigation, talking to witnesses, examining evidence, and speaking to people who knew him. The investigation indicated the man died by suicide, which the medical examiner’s office confirmed, according to Prince George’s County Executive Aisha Braveboy.

# WTOP Maryland 
A medical examiner determined a man found hanging from a tree Sunday morning in Upper Marlboro, Maryland, had committed suicide, officials in Prince George’s County said Monday. County leaders reached that conclusion just hours after it happened.

Still, the symbolism behind the death meant emotions were raw and heightened as county leaders held a press conference — something nearly unheard of when it involves a suicide — to promise honesty and transparency as the investigation continues.

Prince George’s County Police Chief George Nader said that the family agrees with the investigation being ruled as a suicide.

Police said the last time the man was seen alone was 8 p.m. Saturday, prior to the incident. Nader said that the man was seen on video carrying a rope consistent to the one discovered at the scene.

“One thing that I will say, all the attention on this incident is making it more unbearable for the family,” Nader said.

The man did not have any connection to the building where the tree was located, police said.

The body, a Black man whose identity has not been made publicly available, was found hanging from a tree in front of an industrial business in the 9300 block of D’Arcy Road on Sunday morning.

While police and fire responded fast, they couldn’t touch the body until a medical examiner could arrive and conduct their investigation. 

“Our investigation doesn’t stop,” said Maj. Paul Gambardella, commander of the Prince George’s County police’s homicide unit, which was on the scene Sunday. “Whether it’s looking through our decedent’s cellphone, whether it’s following up with any camera footage that we may or may not have, whether it’s talking to anybody else in the neighborhood that we haven’t talked to, this continues even after the ruling.”

He reiterated there was nothing on the scene that suggested there was foul play.
