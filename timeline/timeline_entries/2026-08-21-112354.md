# Stars and Stripes 
The Pentagon on Friday moved to fire the editor in chief of Stars and Stripes as well as its retiring publisher and a civilian reporter who covers the Middle East for the news organization.

Erik Slavin, the editor in chief, said he was being fired for insubordination after stating in a CBS interview last month that censorship of news for service members would constitute a red line in the Pentagon’s efforts to exert control over the newspaper.

“I stand by the principle that Stars and Stripes must remain editorially independent, as required by law and by the department’s own policies,” he said in a statement Friday.

Slavin was named the paper’s top editor in September 2025. He joined Stars and Stripes in 2005 as a reporter in Japan and went on to embed with U.S. forces in Iraq, report in South Korea and oversee news operations in Europe and the Middle East.

He said in the feature on CBS Sunday Morning that it was imperative for Stars and Stripes to provide independent news to troops.

“If we can’t do that, if we were turned into something other than that, if we were public relations,” he said, then that would be something on which he would be unwilling to compromise.

The firings on Friday follow the April termination of the publication’s ombudsman, a role established by Congress to safeguard the paper’s editorial independence.

The Pentagon in March outlined an overhaul effort for Stars and Stripes that barred it from using some news wire services and running comics and other syndicated features.

It also stated that content must be consistent with “good order and discipline,” a phrase borrowed from the Uniform Code of Military Justice.
