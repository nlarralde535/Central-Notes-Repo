# WXBU CBS 21 Harrisburg
After an attempted arrest by U.S. Immigrations Custom Enforcement led to shots fired in Harrisburg, a Pennsylvania immigration attorney is calling for better protocols when it comes to public ICE operations.

Wednesday morning, ICE agents tried to pull over a man at the intersection of S. 17th and Berryhill streets. The agency said the man was from Mexico and in the country illegally.

CBS 21 obtained video from a nearby business that shows the events leading up to the gunfire.

Agents can be seen emerging from unmarked vehicles wearing tactical vests. Two of them walk to the driver’s window, where they repeatedly yell for him to get out of the vehicle, or they’ll break his window.

Seconds later, an agent breaks the driver side window.

The driver then pulls away, appearing to clip the corner of an agent’s vehicle and the front side of a parked car in the process.

In a statement, ICE said the driver, who they identified as Clemente Lara-Hernandez, had “weaponized his car and rammed an ICE law enforcement vehicle” before he “dangerously drove on the wrong direction on a one-way street.”

After the vehicle pulls passed the agents and is driving away, an agent can be seen and heard firing at the vehicle.

Raymond Lahoud, an Allentown-based immigration attorney with 16 years of experience, said he may have acted the same way, given it doesn’t appear any of the agents identified themselves.

"I would be afraid that this is somebody that is not ICE or not a police officer that is just out to get me for some reason or the like,” he said. “Because, I mean, this has never really kind of happened to this extent before."

He called for clearer protocols when it comes to ICE arrests, such as working with local police departments in these kinds of situations, not wearing masks, driving marked vehicles and clearly identifying themselves as law enforcement agents

When asked what rights people have in these kinds of situations, Lahoud paused.

He referenced a 2025 case upheld in the Supreme Court in California—Noem v. Vasquez—where he said it was ruled that ICE agents are allowed to stop people based on their location (places like car washes or day laborer pickup sites), their apparent race and their accents.

“So what rights do you have? I guess the right not to get killed, you know, the right not to get beaten down, the right to some kind of decency when getting pulled over, the right to some kind of procedure,” he said.

Lahoud gave two pieces of advice to people who might find themselves in these kinds of situations.

First, he said it’s better to stay silent than to lie to an agent. Second, get an attorney.

Dauphin County Commissioner Justin Douglas released a statement Thursday evening calling for a “thorough, independent, and transparent investigation.”

Douglas said the actions caught on camera did not appear to adhere to the U.S. Department of Justice’s policy pertaining to the use of deadly force.
