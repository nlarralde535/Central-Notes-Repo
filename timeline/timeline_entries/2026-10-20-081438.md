# Votebeat
The Trump administration’s latest legal assault on California’s elections is raising new concerns about whether the White House is laying groundwork for attempts to challenge the results of the rapidly approaching midterms.

Less than three weeks before Election Day, the Justice Department filed an unusual lawsuit against California that asks a federal judge to set aside as many as 20,000 ballots the federal government claims could be cast by ineligible noncitizen voters. The Trump administration followed up with a letter threatening to prosecute election officials who count votes from potential noncitizens for being “aiders and abettors to a federal crime.”

“This is the first time the Trump administration has tried to take action to sequester ballots that are live ballots — and that’s unusual,” said Stephen Richer, a former Republican registrar in Arizona who now works as a legal fellow at the libertarian think tank Cato Institute. “The fact that this action was taken while this election is underway charts new territory for this administration.”

The move has raised alarms among voting rights groups, but several legal experts questioned whether courts would be willing to support the federal government’s request to segregate the ballots

Michael Morley, an election law expert at the Florida State University College of Law, predicted that the lawsuit would get no traction and that the Justice Department would be unable to intervene in the normal counting process in California.

“If the Trump Administration were attempting to weaponize federal law in an effort to steal an election, California seems like one of the last places that would occur, since you’re not going to be able to challenge or reject enough votes to swing federal races to Republican majorities,” he said.

The dispute centers on the Trump administration’s repeated claims that California’s voter rolls contain tens of thousands of noncitizens who are ineligible to vote. In July, federal officials said they had found more than 190,000 potential noncitizens on California voter rolls and directed the state to scrub any ineligible people from the system.

The Trump administration never provided the state with the list of 190,000 potential noncitizens on the voter rolls. State officials and voting right advocates questioned the administration’s numbers and noted that a check of the administration’s estimated noncitizen voter numbers in other states, such as Nevada, had proven that few, if any, of the names on the list turned out to be noncitizens and raised questions about the administration’s methodology.

Earlier this week, the Justice Department sent Los Angeles County election officials an unusual package: an unsolicited thumb drive said to contain the names of 20,000 people the Trump administration claimed were noncitizens on California voter rolls. It isn’t clear why they sent the information to Los Angeles County, or how the administration’s number of potential noncitizens on the state’s rolls fell from more than 190,000 to 20,000.
