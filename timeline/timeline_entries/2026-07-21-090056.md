# MSN
A federal magistrate judge in Florida on Tuesday, July 21, ordered President Donald Trump to provide financial records sought by the BBC as the broadcaster defends itself against his $10 billion defamation lawsuit.

The ruling came during the first court hearing in the case, which centers on a Panorama documentary about Trump's January 6, 2021 speech before the US Capitol riot. The decision marks an early legal victory for the BBC as both sides continue to dispute the scope of the evidence to be exchanged before trial.

US Magistrate Judge Enjolique Lett ruled that Trump must turn over financial records connected to the Donald J Trump Revocable Trust, concluding that the president's lawsuit places the condition of his businesses directly at issue.

"All of President Trump's brand, properties and businesses are impugned or said to have been impugned. Reputational, economic damages, all of that is now at issue in this case," Lett said at the conclusion of the hearing.

The ruling allows the BBC to seek financial records connected to companies held through Trump's family trust, though Trump can appeal the decision. Trump sued the BBC last year over a Panorama episode, alleging it edited portions of his January 6 speech to falsely suggest he directly encouraged violence during the Capitol riot.

The judge also issued a split ruling on the BBC's efforts to obtain additional records concerning January 6, allowing inquiry into how Trump's remarks were interpreted.
