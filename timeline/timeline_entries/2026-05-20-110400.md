# Houston Chronicle 
The Trump administration’s politically-divisive decision to detain and house immigrant families in Texas is costing at least $13.1 million per month, plus another $2.5 million for medical care, even as the population at the Dilley Immigration Processing Center ebbs and flows.

Most of that money is going to CoreCivic, the company providing everything from security to medical care to box lunches at the facility southwest of San Antonio. Through an agreement that lets the federal government contract with a state, city or county, CoreCivic, the site’s landowner and the City of Dilley can make approximately $15.6 million a month operating the site no matter how many people are detained there, so long as CoreCivic has the capability to house up to 2,400 people.

Lawyers for CoreCivic sent the contract to the Houston Chronicle, as an addendum, when requesting the Texas Attorney General to block it from release. CoreCivic said in a statement that federal officials are “best served to address” details of the contract, which dictates the company notify ICE when asked to comment.

Opened in 2014, the complex was meant to be a temporary home for immigrants as they were either released to await their immigration hearings or held in a place that kept the families together until their cases could be resolved. 

Millions were spent as the population at the center came and went under three administrations. But the money dried up when the original contract with CoreCivic expired in August 2024, and the Biden administration did not renew it. The Trump administration announced the reopening of the facility in March 2025 – later extending the contract to March 2030. 

Based on the contract terms, CoreCivic, City of Dilley and landowner Target Hospitality Group receive payment so long as they are capable of accommodating up to 2,400 people. Whatever the resident population, the companies are paid the monthly rate of $13.1 million — a $437,000 increase with the amended contract in September 2025 — and $2.5 million for medical services.

Upon reopening, CoreCivic said the center could generate $180 million in revenue annually, of which Dilley — pop. 3,064 as of January 2025 — receives less than $1 million.

In its 2025-2026 fiscal budget, the city listed $874,000 as “Core Civic Revenue.” City officials did not respond to questions about those payments.

“Once we factor in the indirect impact, the economic benefits are huge for the area,” said Dilley’s then-city administrator, Henry Arredondo, last year when the reopening was announced.

The property is owned by Target Hospitality Group, which essentially rents the site to CoreCivic. In a March 2025 announcement reopening the site, Target said it expected to earn $246 million through the contract’s March 2030 expiration.
