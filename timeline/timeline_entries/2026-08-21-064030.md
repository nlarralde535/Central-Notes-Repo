# WBUR Boston
Activists had said they planned to use the court case to highlight detention conditions inside ICE’s New England field office.

In September, Nathan Phillips and Nastasia Lawton-Sticklor tried to deliver food to immigrants at the ICE facility in Burlington, following months of revelations about the office being used to house detainees for multiple days. They were arrested and charged by Burlington Police with misdemeanor trespassing.

Instead of striking a deal with prosecutors, the pair decided to go to trial and bring witnesses to testify about the controversial facility.

The trial was scheduled to begin Aug. 26, nearly a year after the arrest. But on Wednesday, Middlesex District Attorney Marian Ryan’s office told the court it was dropping the charges.

Kylah Clay, an attorney with the National Lawyers Guild representing Phillips, said she was surprised by the decision — and its timing.

“I assume that once they saw that we were going to be calling some really important witnesses to testify, they decided it wasn't worth it for them to go to trial,” she said.
