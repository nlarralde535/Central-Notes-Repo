# WCTV Tallahassee
A Chattahoochee Police officer has been fired for allegedly tasing a man experiencing a medical emergency and mistreating an emergency medical services employee.

Dwight Jackson, a Chattahoochee Street resident, was outside putting up Halloween decorations Sunday when he suddenly had a seizure and fell to the ground.

His brother, Caleb Johns, called for medical help and said Jackson’s seizure had lasted for about 10 minutes before EMS arrived.

They said about 45 minutes after Jackson received aid, a Chattahoochee Police officer arrived and jumped on top of him with a taser.

“They are all still rendering aid to him; they are already holding him down, they pretty much got it under control, this is just now when he’s starting to come back to. And in that moment, that’s when Price started; he shoves everyone out the way and jumps on top of Dwight,” Johns said.

That’s when Johns pulled out his phone and started recording.

The video was posted on social media and has since gained thousands of views.

Jackson says the experience left him terrified.

“I think the dude would kill me, I really feel like that would have either beat me up or tried to shoot me if that taser didn’t work the next time,” Jackson said.

On Wednesday, Police Chief Michael Miller said EMS was having trouble restraining a “combative individual.” He said Sgt. Price arrived to help and encountered “physical resistance,” claiming Jackson pulled on the officer’s body armor.

But Jackson said he wasn’t combative; he was experiencing a seizure.

“I’ve never experienced in my life something like this right here,” Jackson said. “That’s why I said that was more terrifying than being in the war because it’s unexpected; I’m defenseless.”

Miller’s statement also addresses a second video showing Price getting into a verbal altercation with an EMS employee.

Miller said the officer’s conduct and demeanor did not meet the department’s standards and Price was fired.

Miller said the department recognizes the concerns raised by the incident and that he holds officers accountable.
