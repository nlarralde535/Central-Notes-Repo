# Houston Chronicle 
A Hearst Newspapers analysis of the standards found that students would encounter Christian religious concepts at every grade level from kindergarten to senior year. Students will have to read 17 Bible stories or passages from across the Old and New Testaments. And they will need to learn about scores of new religious figures, including Jesus, Abraham and Moses, as well as major events in Christian history.

The Republican-led board will take a final vote in June, and if approved, the standards would enter classrooms and be incorporated into standardized testing in 2030.

Conservative proponents of the plan say the changes will help Texas students understand the influences of Christianity on the country’s culture and foundations. But critics, including historians and Democrats on the state board, warn the new standards violate constitutional principles and essentially mandate a state religion. They say some of the requirements are not even historically accurate.

Current social studies standards ask students to trace the development of religious freedom in the United States and require high schoolers to analyze major world religions. The new proposals would center Christianity within American history and greatly boost overtly faith-based content, while trimming in-depth lessons of other religions.
