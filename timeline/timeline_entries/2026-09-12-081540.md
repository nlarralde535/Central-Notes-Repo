# KDFW Fox 4 News
Trinidad PD ordered to disband

The Texas Commission on Law Enforcement sent a letter to Trinidad's mayor saying the police department "failed to meet minimum standards" and "was unable to provide evidence that the agency provides public benefit to the community."

Police department lacked resources, policies

TCOLE's letter to the mayor stated the agency found the police department lacked resources for officers, including not having bulletproof vests or an evidence room. 

They were also found to not have nearly two dozen necessary policies, including those addressing use of force, active shooter situations, vehicle pursuits and professional conduct of officers.

# The Dallas Express
The department came under intense scrutiny after the May arrest of citizen journalist Jennifer Combs over a Facebook post concerning Trinidad’s troubled water system.

Combs was charged with felony false alarm or report after posting that she had received reports of people being hospitalized because of the city’s water. A Henderson County grand jury later declined to indict her.

The controversy expanded the following day when citizen journalist Winston Noles was arrested during a protest outside Trinidad City Hall. Noles was charged with misdemeanor disorderly conduct over a sign containing expletives directed at police. A municipal judge later dismissed the charge.

The city subsequently fired Municipal Judge Shellena Bivens, who had dismissed Noles’ charge. Bivens said she was considering legal action and characterized her removal as retaliation.

Further scrutiny followed when Henderson County District Judge R. Scott McKee, who had approved a search warrant connected to Combs’ arrest, questioned the information provided by two Trinidad police officers.

McKee wrote that he later questioned the “accuracy, completeness, and reliability” of information that was presented to him at the time that he approved the warrant.

Former Police Chief Charles Gregory resigned effective June 19 following the controversy. Interim Chief Cameron Beckham subsequently told FOX 4 that he believed problems within the department went back years.

Beckham said he would have referred the Combs and Noles cases to the District Attorney’s Office rather than filing the charges directly.

Water Crisis Remains Part of Broader Dispute

The police department’s problems unfolded alongside continuing concerns about Trinidad’s water system.

A Texas Commission on Environmental Quality report released in July detailed problems involving aging water distribution mains and system design. The report also identified chemical levels more than 300% above the federally allowed limit in certain testing.

The water controversy has also resulted in lawsuits and additional leadership changes within the city.
