# Stars and Stripes 
The Pentagon’s firing last week of three employees of the newspaper Stars and Stripes was done out of retaliation over unflattering coverage of a Navy deployment and in violation of the protected right to free speech, according to a lawsuit filed Thursday in Washington.

Erik Slavin, the editor in chief; Lara Korte, the paper’s Middle East reporter; and Max D. Lederer Jr., the publisher, filed the 45-page lawsuit against chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell, the Defense Department, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and another public affairs official, Andrew Brey.

It asks the court to halt their terminations as unlawful and seeks an immediate restraining order.

The legal team representing the journalists is the same that represents employees of Voice of America, another government-owned news outlet that the Trump Administration has attempted to shut down. Both have mandates to give propaganda-free news, said David Seide, an attorney on the case with the Government Accountability Project.

Voice of America employees have two lawsuits ongoing against the government — one regarding their employment and the other about violations to its First Amendment protections.
