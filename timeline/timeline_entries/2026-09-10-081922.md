# NBC News
Joe diGenova, the 81-year-old Trump loyalist who had headed a sprawling investigation in the Southern District of Florida that targeted President Donald Trump’s perceived political enemies, has resigned from the Justice Department.

“It was an honor and a privilege to serve the president and the department,” diGenova said Thursday.

Asked why he is leaving, diGenova declined to comment.

“I’m moving on,” he said.

ABC News first reported diGenova’s departure.

While diGenova resigned from the Justice Department and was not fired, a source familiar with the determination told NBC News that Justice Department officials had already lost confidence in his leadership skills and his ability to run the investigation.

DiGenova, a top federal prosecutor under President Ronald Reagan, was part of Trump’s efforts to overturn the 2020 election in his favor. He made headlines this year when it was revealed that he would head the Florida-based investigation into Trump’s foes.

DiGenova’s probe has not produced any prosecutions, though shortly before he resigned there was a recent burst of activity in the wide investigation. The Justice Department is seeking testimony from multiple former officials before a federal grand jury, people familiar with the probe told NBC News. The Associated Press first reported the subpoena activity.

The probe reached back a decade, revisiting a Justice Department inquiry into Russian interference in the 2016 presidential election. That posed a major hurdle for the investigation, given that the activity took place nearly a decade ago, far outside the typical five-year statute of limitations for most federal crimes.

DiGenova stepped in to replace career prosecutor Maria Medetis Long, who had objected to advancing an investigation into former CIA Director John Brennan, whom Trump allies were pushing to be charged for allegedly false testimony.
