# Religion News
Martín Alonso Soto Hernandez, a Peruvian asylum seeker who was held in solitary confinement at Elizabeth Contract Detention Facility after helping organize a hunger and labor strike at Delaney Hall, was released from ICE custody around 8 p.m. Tuesday evening (Oct. 6). His immigration case remains ongoing. 

Soto, wearing an ankle monitor, was reunited with his wife, Gabriela Soto — who has become a vocal immigrant rights advocate — and his two young children.  

The Sotos, who live in Kearny, New Jersey, married in 2024 and attended a church in Newark before Martín Soto’s arrest in early 2026, according to Gabriela Soto, who is a U.S. citizen. She became active in rallies outside Delaney Hall after her husband was detained there in early 2026, saying her husband was served food contaminated with worms and was not receiving adequate medical care. The Department of Homeland Security and the GEO Group, a private company that operates Delaney Hall, have denied allegations of mistreatment at the facility.

On May 22, local news reported, Gabriela Soto organized a rally to call attention to the hunger and labor strike and demand the release of everyone in detention. Clergy and immigrant rights activists rallied around Martín Soto’s case. Two days later, according to NJ.com, Martín Soto was transferred to Elizabeth Detention Center. Activists and faith leaders described the move as retaliation for his involvement in the strike. He later was placed in solitary confinement and, according to Gabriela Soto, his mental health suffered significantly.

DHS did not respond to questions about whether they held Martín Soto in solitary confinement or if it was in retaliation for the hunger strike. Instead, an ICE spokesperson wrote in an email that he is “charged with assault,” helped incite “unrest” at Delaney Hall and would receive due process. Martín Soto’s attorney, Selenia Destefani, told RNS the assault case was dismissed. She added that they are challenging his deportation order and pursuing various options for obtaining legal status.
