# Government Accountability Office
Millions of American adults earn so little that they rely on federal programs to make ends meet, such as Medicaid and the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program.

To learn more about the working adults who used these programs in 2024, we analyzed employment data from 11 states and Census data.

We found:

- About two-thirds worked full time
- Most worked for private sector employers
- Workers were largely in transportation, restaurants and food prep, and retail sales jobs
- Some employers in selected states had thousands of beneficiaries on their payrolls

# GAO Report
In summary, we found the following:

• According to Census data, millions of wage-earning adults enrolled in Medicaid or living
in households that received SNAP food assistance shared common labor
characteristics. These shared characteristics included working predominantly for private-
sector employers, working mostly full-time work schedules, and being highly
concentrated in five industries and occupations. Among adults who worked at some
point during 2024, an estimated 13.8 million were enrolled in Medicaid, and 10.6 million
lived in households receiving SNAP benefits, according to CPS ASEC program
participation data.5 Wage-earning adult Medicaid enrollees and SNAP recipients were
more likely than wage earners who did not participate in the programs to work in the
private sector; work in the arts, entertainment, and recreation, and accommodation and
food services industries; and work in the food service and food preparation occupations.
• Nondisabled, nonelderly (NDNE) working adult Medicaid enrollees made up between 15
and 62 percent of all adult Medicaid enrollees in the six states that provided data.
Working adult SNAP recipients made up between 13 and 35 percent of adult SNAP
recipients in the nine states that provided data. These working Medicaid enrollees and
SNAP recipients worked overwhelmingly in the private sector.
• According to our analysis of state-level data from September 2025, the employers who
employed the most Medicaid enrollees or SNAP beneficiaries varied across the 11 states
that provided data. However, 46 companies (about one-third of the total companies
across all 11 states) were among the top 25 employers of Medicaid enrollees or SNAP
beneficiaries in at least two states. Seventeen of these 46 companies were among the
50 largest Fortune 500 companies by number of employees.
