# Politico
The Trump administration moved Friday to temporarily lift sanctions on Russian diesel as President Donald Trump said Moscow had agreed to release large quantities of the fuel and suggested the U.S. could be a buyer.

The surprise announcement is the Trump administration’s latest effort in its struggle to bring the record diesel prices — caused by the ongoing war against Iran as well as Ukrainian attacks against Russian refineries — under control ahead of the midterm elections. It also could signal weakening of Trump’s support for Ukraine in its fight against Russia’s invasion, Democratic lawmakers said.

In a post on Truth Social, Trump said he had just “concluded a highly successful discussion” with Russian President Vladimir Putin, in which Putin agreed to supply hundreds of thousands of tons of diesel fuel “to the American and Global Marketplace.” Russia would “immediately supply” the fuel “based on the condition of their Diesel Refineries,” Trump added.

Immediately after Trump’s post, the Treasury Department issued a general license allowing for the “sale, delivery, offloading, or importation, including importation into the United States” of Russian diesel through April 2027.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy accused Trump of using peace talks in Florida between U.S. and Ukrainian negotiators as a “smokescreen” while the U.S. struck a deal with Russia.

Trump last week ruled out an export ban on diesel, instead securing a pledge by European allies to tap their diesel stockpiles, though those governments have since said the announcement largely amounted to preexisting commitments. Trump has previously criticized European allies for continuing to buy Russian oil that was helping to fund its continued campaign against Ukraine. Some European countries insisted the U.S. uphold its sanctions against Russia as a condition for the release, according to minutes from last Friday’s EU ambassadors meeting.

Trump’s diesel deal with Russia comes less than a month after he signed the massive Russia sanctions legislation dedicated to the memory of Sen. Lindsey Graham and that allows for tariffs against buyers of Russian oil. The news that Trump would effectively ignore the legislation and possibly buy fuel from Russia directly raised alarm among top Democrats.

Russia has restricted diesel exports in recent months as Ukraine has accelerated its attacks on its refineries, contributing to the global shortage of the fuel. It is unclear how Moscow will supply the quantities of the fuel promised by Trump with a large chunk of its refineries out of commission.

Energy experts quickly expressed skepticism that the announcement would make a dent in diesel prices.

Gregory Brew, a senior analyst at Eurasia Group, said Moscow’s immediate commitment amounts to less than 2% of the total U.S. output of 3.6 million barrels per day.

“Beyond the fact that these numbers aren’t very big, this would indicate Russia is willing to end its ban on refined products exports...which I have a hard time seeing without a pause in Ukrainian attacks,” Brew wrote on X.

The Kremlin has yet to comment publicly on Trump’s announcement. But Putin’s special presidential representative for investment and economic cooperation, Kirill Dmitriev, celebrated the move within minutes of Trump’s announcement Friday.
