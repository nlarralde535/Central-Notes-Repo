# Houston Chronicle 
Houston ISD’s state-appointed board of managers voted unanimously Thursday to adopt the state-developed Bluebonnet curriculum, which has drawn controversy for its inclusion of Bible teachings but could bring the district millions in new state funding.

Bluebonnet is a suite of state-owned free instructional materials and comes with extra financial incentives, up to $60 per student. HISD expects to receive $3.3 million from the state by adopting the curriculum for kindergarten through fifth grades. 

HISD is the latest district to adopt the materials. One-third of Texas school districts have ordered the Bible-infused lesson plans, and 20 of those 367 districts are in the Houston area, according to data obtained at the start of the 2025-26 school year. 

The district plans to use Bluebonnet English and Spanish reading and language arts materials for kindergarten through fifth grade, along with other materials including district-developed curriculum. In a Friday statement, HISD said it listed K-5 Bluebonnet math as an option for campuses outside of the New Education System in a submission to the state, "but HISD has no plans to use Bluebonnet math in its curriculum."

Experts say the Bluebonnet reading curriculum places an outsized emphasis on Christianity and puts teachers in a difficult situation of being asked questions about religion that they may not know how to answer. Some districts have avoided adopting Bluebonnet because of the printing requirement or because, they said, the materials were not rigorous enough.

Districts that choose from the state's approved list of high-quality instructional materials can receive an extra $40 per enrolled student. And if a district adopts Bluebonnet, they receive an extra $20 for printing the materials, totaling $60 per student. But Bluebonnet materials are designed to be printed, and, in some cases, the $60-per-student incentive did not cover the full printing cost.
