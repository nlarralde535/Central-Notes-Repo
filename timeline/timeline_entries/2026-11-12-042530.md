# Military Times
The House passed a major veterans benefits package on Wednesday, advancing new support for surviving spouses and caregivers, as well as expanded compensation for some disabled retirees, while — controversially — tying the legislation’s financing to changes to future benefits.

The Take Care of America’s Veterans Act passed by voice vote on Veterans Day without a recorded tally of how individual lawmakers voted. The package includes more than 60 provisions, but its most contentious measure would change how the Department of Veterans Affairs rates sleep apnea and tinnitus, potentially reducing future disability compensation for some veterans.

The funding approach has divided veterans organizations that support many of the bill’s benefit expansions but disagree over whether veterans’ future benefits should help pay for them.

Section 108 of the bill would establish new VA disability-rating criteria for sleep apnea and tinnitus.

Under the proposed schedule, asymptomatic sleep apnea would receive a 0% rating, whether or not the veteran is receiving treatment. A 10% rating would apply when treatment provides incomplete relief. A 50% rating would be available only when treatment is ineffective or a qualifying medical condition prevents its use, provided there is no end-organ damage. A 100% rating would require end-organ damage.

The bill would eliminate separate compensable ratings for tinnitus, or ringing in the ears, while retaining a 10% rating in a specified circumstance involving service-connected hearing loss.

Meanwhile, Section 104 increases the cost for some VA-backed home loans

Supporters say projected savings from the disability-rating changes and revised VA home loan fees would help finance the bill’s expanded benefits.

The American Legion and the Tragedy Assistance Program for Survivors were among 23 organizations that signed a June 29 letter urging Congress to advance the legislative package while acknowledging concerns about offsets from future disability compensation. The organizations said they would prefer the benefits without those offsets but supported moving the package forward while pressing for protections and alternatives.

Disabled American Veterans called for Section 108 to be removed. The VFW has opposed the bill as drafted, citing the rating changes, fee increases and other reductions, while supporting many of its benefit expansions.

Iraq and Afghanistan Veterans of America also opposed the package. CEO Kyleanne Hunter said Congress should not establish a precedent for reducing earned disability compensation to finance other programs.
