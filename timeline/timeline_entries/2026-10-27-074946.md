# WZTV 17 Nashville
According to the Cleveland Clinic, rhabdomyolysis is a condition that causes your muscles to break down (disintegrate), which leads to muscle death. When this happens, toxic components of your muscle fibers enter your circulatory system and kidneys. This can cause kidney damage.

Darius Chappell, 34, died in the Montgomery County Jail four days after a Clarksville police K9 attack during an arrest in which he was also tased multiple times, back in June, following a pursuit by law enforcement.

Rhabdomyolysis can happen when muscles are badly damaged. As the muscle tissue breaks down, it releases substances into the blood, including a protein called myoglobin. Myoglobin can damage the kidneys.

Chappell’s autopsy found myoglobin in his kidney tubules, brown urine and elevated kidney-related measurements. The medical examiner cited those findings as evidence supporting the cause of death.

On June 29, 2026, Montgomery County sheriff’s deputies tried to arrest Chappell on a felony parole violation warrant stemming from a drug charge six years prior, according to the Sheriff’s Office. The office said he ran from deputies and physically resisted arrest. Clarksville police assisted.

Cellphone video reviewed by FOX 17 News shows officers struggling with Chappell, who was tased multiple times. A Clarksville police officer then deployed a K-9 while Chappell was already on the ground. The dog appears to bite him for nearly a minute as multiple officers work to restrain him.

Chappell was later charged with evading and resisting arrest, and assault on a first responder.

FOX17 spoke exclusively to Chappell's mother Latoshia Phinnessee.

"I just dont understand why it took so many people (during the arrest), because he wasn't resisting," she questioned. "Why did it take so many to get a person down that had his hands behind his back?... I just don't understand... why they had so much anger... There was rage. There's was anger."

The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation is investigating Chappell’s death independently from the Clarksville Police Department and Montgomery County Sheriff's Office. Clarksville PD said Officer Steven Deering was terminated effective Sept. 1 after an internal investigation and disciplinary process related to the K-9 use of force during the incident.
