# KPLC 7 Louisiana
Leaders in a Louisiana community are responding to the likelihood that a candidate accused of raping two children is set to become their next police chief.

Elizabeth, a village in Allen Parish with around 400 residents, is scheduled to elect its next police chief in November. One of the candidates was recently arrested on rape charges, and now the other has announced that he is stepping down from the race.

Chris Paul was arrested in early September after he was accused of raping two 13-year-old girls 20 years ago. He faces two counts of second-degree rape.

Elizabeth Mayor Mandy Green reminded residents in a Facebook post that Paul has not been convicted of a crime, and he is presumed innocent until proven guilty. She also said she and the village’s Board of Aldermen have no authority to change the ballot under state law.

But a big change could also be coming to how Elizabeth chooses its police chief.

During a special-called meeting Friday afternoon, the village’s Board of Aldermen approved an ordinance to put an item on the Dec. 12 ballot that would change police chief from an elected position to an appointed position, if voted in.

Whether the proposition stemmed from the current situation or when it would go into effect is unclear. KPLC reached out to village officials, but they declined comment or did not return our phone calls.

Paul’s opponent, Zachary LaCaze, said he withdrew after learning that his candidacy violated the Hatch Act because he works for the federal government, and while LaCaze is running as a nonpartisan candidate, Paul is running as a Republican. LaCaze said when asked to choose between his job and the race, he decided to keep his job to continue supporting his family.
