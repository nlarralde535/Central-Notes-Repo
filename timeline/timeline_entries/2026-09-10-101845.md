# Middle East Monitor
Israeli left-wing newspaper Haaretz rejected three demands Thursday by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to avoid being sued and vowed to reveal the truth in court, Anadolu reports.

The dispute concerns a report published Tuesday by the newspaper that said Netanyahu received a warning from United Arab Emirates President Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan in a call before Oct. 7, 2023, that Hamas was planning to launch an attack.

Netanyahu denied having any contact with the UAE president or receiving any warning from him. The UAE Foreign Ministry said it does not comment on reports of talks between government leaders.

Haaretz said in a statement that it recently sent a response to Netanyahu’s lawyers rejecting his threat to sue the newspaper and journalists Shlomi Eldar and Ruti Yuval.

The newspaper said the report concerned a warning from the UAE president to Netanyahu 10 days before the Oct. 7 attack about a major operation that late Hamas leader Yahya Sinwar was planning against Israel.

The Palestinian resistance group, Hamas, attacked military bases and settlements near the Gaza Strip, killing and capturing Israelis.

Israeli military, intelligence and political officials have described the attack as an unprecedented political, security and military failure in Israel’s history.

While several security, military and political officials have acknowledged responsibility for the failure, Netanyahu has rejected responsibility and instead blamed the military.

Haaretz said the report was “the product of an in-depth, well-sourced journalistic investigation into a matter of the utmost public interest.”

The newspaper told Netanyahu’s lawyer that“in the absence of full accountability to the public on behalf of your client, the press is fulfilling its role and revealing information to the public, piece by piece.”

Netanyahu ignored the “UAE warning” about the possibility of a Hamas attack and did not inform the military or the Shin Bet security agency, according to the newspaper.

While declining to comment on the report by Haaretz, the UAE Foreign Ministry said that Abu Dhabi and Tel Aviv have maintained “open and direct channels of communication” since establishing relations more than five years ago, through which they exchange intelligence when necessary.

# Times of Israel
Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Wednesday he would sue the Haaretz daily and called for a criminal probe over its report that said he had been warned by the Emirati president about Hamas plans to attack Israel days before the October 7, 2023, onslaught and didn’t act on the information.

Netanyahu reasserted on X that there had been no call between him and Emirati President Mohammed bin Zayed between early September 2023 and the October 7 invasion. He argued that any such call would have left clear documentation, which he said has not been found by the Prime Minister’s Office team.

Later Wednesday, Netanyahu shared a statement by his Likud party, which accused Haaretz and Democrats party leader Yair Golan of “blatant foreign interference in Israel’s elections,” and called for a criminal investigation against Golan and the journalists who wrote the story.

The statement did not cite any evidence for its claims, including Golan’s alleged involvement in the article’s publication. It claimed that the report was politically motivated.

Netanyahu’s party also raised the possibility, again without evidence, that Golan and former Shin Bet chief Ronen Bar’s recent reported visit to the UAE could be connected to the Haaretz report.

“Likud demands the opening of a criminal investigation against Shlomi Eldar, Ruth Yuval, the newspaper Haaretz, Yair Golan, and the Democrats party,” said Netanyahu’s post, “due to suspicion of attempting to disrupt the elections with foreign entities.”

In response to the threats of lawsuit and the call for a criminal probe, Haaretz said it stood by its reporting.

An Emirati source familiar with the conversation confirmed the Haaretz newspaper’s report to The Times of Israel on Tuesday.

Netanyahu’s election rival Naftali Bennett said after publication of the Haaretz report that it was “true.” The former prime minister did not offer any details on how he knew this, though he is known to be friendly with bin Zayed. Channel 13 reported on Wednesday that Bennett had indeed told people in private conversations that bin Zayed had informed him of the call.

According to Tuesday’s Haaretz report, bin Zayed warned Netanyahu in a 45-minute phone call in late September that Hamas leader Yahya Sinwar was planning major action against Israel that would lead to bloodshed, unsettle the region and potentially harm the Abraham Accords.
