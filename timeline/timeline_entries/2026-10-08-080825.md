# Ynet News 
David Milstein, a senior adviser to U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee, repeatedly blocked or substantially altered reports critical of Israel in an effort to present Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s government in a more favorable light to the administration in Washington, a New York Times investigation published Thursday alleges.

For the investigation, Times reporters interviewed 15 current U.S. officials who had worked with Milstein in some capacity, as well as nine former diplomats who helped provide background. The newspaper also obtained or reviewed dozens of documents, cables, memos and other internal communications that it said showed Milstein’s involvement in changing embassy reporting.
Most of those who spoke to the Times said they did so out of concern that Milstein’s actions were undermining standard diplomatic work. Some also expressed concern that, in certain cases, his actions contradicted President Donald Trump’s stated policies.

One example cited in the investigation took place in late March, at the height of the war with Iran, when Israel quietly approved more West Bank settlements at once than ever before. A sharply worded, urgent report on the move never reached the U.S. administration. According to three U.S. officials, Milstein blocked it.

A similar case occurred in April, when senior IDF officials warned that settler violence in the West Bank could ignite a new Palestinian uprising. Embassy personnel tried to relay those warnings to Washington, but Milstein prevented the reports from being sent in their original form, according to three officials.

Instead, Milstein praised Netanyahu’s “new and unprecedented initiatives” to combat violence in the West Bank. The report was titled: “Government Acts Forcefully Against Escalating Violence in the West Bank.”

Another case involved members of Congress who requested information about the deaths of nine Palestinian Americans in the West Bank over the past four years. According to multiple officials, Milstein blocked a detailed report prepared by embassy staff, effectively thwarting lawmakers’ efforts to obtain the information.

The main findings in the blocked report showed that in most cases the investigations had made very little progress, that some files had been closed and that no one had been prosecuted over the deaths, according to those officials. Instead of sending the report, Milstein argued that the embassy should send a letter stating that Israel was handling the investigations properly

Officials who spoke with the Times said that, shortly after returning to the embassy in Jerusalem at the start of Trump’s second term in the White House, Milstein began insisting on approving outgoing correspondence from the embassy section dealing with Palestinian affairs.

The volume of reporting fell from several reports a week to only a handful a month, because some staff members stopped writing reports out of fear that Milstein would block or rewrite them, those officials said.

According to several officials, Milstein’s interventions went far beyond those of any of his predecessors. They and others said they were speaking out because they feared officials in Washington were receiving a distorted picture of Israeli actions through key diplomatic channels.
Milstein’s efforts, they said, helped shield the Netanyahu government from potentially harsher responses by the Trump administration
