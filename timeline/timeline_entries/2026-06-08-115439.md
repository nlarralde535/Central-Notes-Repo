# Factually.co
1. How ICE initiates verification: warrants, site visits and the legal basis

ICE operations typically begin with a planned site visit or arrest operation based on investigations or referrals; agents may present administrative warrants, arrest warrants, or civil immigration documents before attempting to verify status, and such enforcement is grounded in federal immigration statutes and agency mission statements posted by ICE [1] [4] [5].

2. Document inspection and the central role of Form I‑9 at worksites

At workplace raids, ICE routinely reviews employers’ I-9 Employment Eligibility Verification forms and related hiring records to match names and documents against individuals present, a practice rooted in the IRCA framework that places a compliance burden on employers and has been used historically to identify unauthorized workers [2] [6].

3. Databases and cross‑checks: paper to digital verification

Agents cross-check presented documents with federal databases — immigration records, prior removal or arrest histories, and other governmental systems — and ICE guidance and legal analyses describe administrative audits and data queries as a standard part of verification during inspections or raids [1] [4] [7].

4. Biometrics and new technology: Mobile Fortify and facial recognition

Since at least 2026, DHS disclosed use of an app called Mobile Fortify that performs facial-comparison and fingerprint-matching against “trusted source photos,” enabling ICE and CBP to scan faces or fingerprints during encounters and compare them to database images; advocates and some news outlets have flagged privacy, accuracy and civil‑liberties concerns about these surveillance tools [3].

5. How ICE handles uncertainty: detentions, mistaken holds and legal process

When identity or status is unresolved on site, ICE may detain an individual for further verification and initiation of removal proceedings under sections of immigration law; reporting and legal guides note that processing delays can lead to U.S. citizens or lawful residents being mistakenly held and that due process issues—access to counsel, interpreter services, and timely hearings—are frequent points of legal challenge [4] [8].

6. Employer-facing verification tools and limits: E‑Verify, audits and enforcement focus

Employers often rely on E‑Verify and I‑9 recordkeeping to demonstrate lawful hiring, but ICE has publicly stated that E‑Verify can be imperfect in enforcement encounters and has continued to increase I‑9 audits and targeted compliance actions; law-firm and industry advisories encourage internal audits and counsel to prepare for inspections, while civil‑rights groups warn that these employer-facing systems can encourage discriminatory screening and still miss people who lack records [9] [10] [6].
