# Hoodline
Houston City Council could vote as soon as Wednesday on whether to apply for a piece of a $300 million federal grant program that prioritizes immigration law enforcement operations and requires coordination with state and federal law enforcement partners. The decision lands in the middle of an ongoing fight over how closely Houston police should cooperate with federal immigration agents, a fight that has already produced lawsuits, a threatened funding cutoff from the state, and a tense showdown inside City Hall.

The grant in question is the Department of Justice's Model Cities Initiative, which the agency launched in June 2026 with a slogan of “Make America Safe Again,” offering competitive awards to two to four U.S. cities with populations over 100,000 that commit to a whole-of-city approach to public safety, according to Houston Public Media. Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche has said the initiative will help the administration “strengthen law enforcement partners and restore the rule of law,” and that the government is using its authority to ensure Americans' safety. The program can fund officer hiring and retention, equipment and technology, victim services, and youth crime prevention, but immigration law enforcement operations are among the Justice Department's stated priorities for the money, and the initiative requires joint coordination among mayors, sheriffs, and prosecutors to qualify, according to the Department of Justice.

# Houston Public Media 
The Houston City Council was slated to consider an application for a "Model Cities Initiative" grant administered by the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) on Wednesday — but the item was pulled from the agenda before the meeting.

Under the grant guidelines, cities "must ensure that their initiatives align" with a number of DOJ priorities, "including immigration law enforcement operations."

In response to questions about the application, a spokesperson for Mayor John Whitmire said the city was "reviewing the possibility of applying for a grant as part of the Model City Initiative."

Recent surges of federal law enforcement in cities like Memphis, Tennessee, and Washington,
D.C. have demonstrated the successes that the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) and the
Trump Administration have had when resources are laser focused on providing cities with the
tools they need to reduce violence, crime, and disorder. For example, since September 29,
2025, overall violent crime in Memphis has plummeted by 42%—murders are down 40%,
robberies are down 50%, sexual assaults are down 35%, and motor vehicle thefts are down
65%. The surge in Washington, D.C. has also resulted in historic outcomes for public safety—
homicide is down 54%, robbery is down 40%, burglary is down 26%, and motor vehicle theft is
down 55%.
