# Out Front Magazine 
Rihanna Kelver, a transgender woman, is facing up to 15 years in prison and $11,000 in fines following an altercation outside the Crowbar & Grill. Despite Wyoming’s strict “Stand Your Ground” statute, local prosecutors are pursuing two heavy felony charges against her: aggravated assault and possession of a deadly weapon with unlawful intent.

The incident began when a man, later identified in court proceedings as Scott Durham, approached Kelver outside the establishment and began shouting homophobic and transphobic slurs. According to court testimony, surveillance footage, and investigative affidavits, the situation escalated rapidly when Durham forcefully shoved Kelver to the pavement, causing her to sustain a tailbone injury. Video evidence reviewed by journalists and detailed in court documents painted a stark picture of the confrontation, showing that Kelver was “alone, outnumbered, physically assaulted and left on the ground facing multiple aggressors.” Durham himself later admitted to investigators that the altercation was a “three-on-one” situation.

In response to being knocked down, Kelver drew a pistol from her bag, chambered a round, and pointed it at Durham, causing him and his companions to flee. Surveillance records show that Kelver kept the weapon’s safety on and never fired a shot. Wyoming law explicitly protects individuals who use force to defend themselves, stating that “a person who uses reasonable defensive force … shall not be criminally prosecuted for that use of reasonable defensive force.” Yet, a pretrial judge ultimately sided with the prosecution, ruling that the felony charges were valid and forcing the case to trial. Durham currently faces no criminal charges for his role in the assault.

The legal trajectory of the case has been highly volatile. In November, Albany County Attorney Kurt Britzius initially lowered the felony charges to two misdemeanors: reckless endangering and interference with a peace officer (a charge Kelver faces for an additional year and a $1,000 fine). Following the reduction, Kelver submitted a formal letter to Judge Robert Sanford to express her regrets over how the night unfolded, writing:

“I do not wish to spend any time attempting to garner sympathy nor victimhood. I wish to offer my sincerest apologies and condolences to your court and to my community.”

However, following the submission of the letter, plea negotiations abruptly collapsed, prompting prosecutors to fully reinstate the original felony charges in March

Kelver’s defense attorney, Andrew Holcomb, emphasized that the upcoming trial is fundamentally a matter of equal constitutional protections. “I think that it’s important we all have the same rights, not only the Second Amendment right to defend yourself, but we all have the right to due process and trial,” Holcomb stated, adding that he is focused on ensuring his client’s rights are protected throughout the remainder of the legal process.

Reported by Kent Morlan

Rihanna Kelver, who is transgender, age 28, drew her gun after she had been pushed down in the street during a brief confrontation with three men.

Wyoming does not require gun owners to register their weapons and allows people to carry firearms concealed or in the open without a permit. The state’s Firearms Freedom Act declares that guns made and kept within state lines are exempt from federal regulations, and its “stand your ground” law allows people to use force to defend themselves if they reasonably believe their safety is threatened. The law says they do not have a duty to retreat if they are not the initial aggressor.

# Wansom.ai
Ríhanna Kelver, a 28-year-old transgender woman from Wyoming, was charged with aggravated assault and possession of a deadly weapon with unlawful intent after pulling a gun on three men who allegedly used gay slurs and pushed her to the ground outside the Crowbar & Grill in Laramie. According to Kelver's account, she was standing outside smoking a cigarette before her shift started when the men shouted obscenities and slurs about her and the bar. The incident occurred in September, and the police report does not identify the three men involved. Video footage of the incident exists, but it lacks audio. Police reviewed the footage, which shows Kelver being pushed to the ground after removing what appeared to be a gun from her satchel. The man who pushed Kelver told police that he was not trying to start a fight, and none of the men were charged in connection with the incident.

Relevant Legal/Regulatory Context

Wyoming has a stand-your-ground law, which allows individuals to use reasonable force in self-defense without a duty to retreat. The law applies when an individual is attacked in a location where they have a right to be and are not the initial aggressor. However, the law also requires that the force used must not be excessive or unlawful. In this case, Kelver's decision to pull a gun on the three men raises questions about whether she reasonably believed herself to be in imminent danger. Wyoming has no permits required for purchasing or carrying guns, which may have contributed to the ease with which Kelver was able to access her firearm during the incident.

# The Laramie Reporter
Ríhanna Kelver was smoking a cigarette before her late-night shift at the Crowbar when one man in a group of men across the street shouted at her.

Accounts vary as to what, exactly, he shouted, but it kicked off a rapidfire sequence of events that would injure Kelver’s spine, land her in jail for the next two nights, and net her a pair of felony charges she now plans to fight.

Kelver has been charged with aggravated assault and possession of a deadly weapon with unlawful intent. If convicted on both counts, Kelver could face up to 15 years in prison.

On Tuesday, Albany County Circuit Court Judge Robert Sanford ruled the felonies can be tried in district court. He rejected a request from the defendant’s lawyer to dismiss the charges outright.

Kelver told The Laramie Reporter she acted in self-defense, fearing in the moment that she could become the victim of a hate crime. As Kelver told police the night of the incident and told the Reporter during an interview before her preliminary hearing, the confrontation began when the man across the street allegedly shouted anti-LGBTQ+ and anti-transgender slurs. Kelver said he was shouting at both her and the Crowbar.

Kelver is a transgender woman. She has been a vocal advocate for trans rights as state legislators have passed laws limiting the healthcare, public access and privacy of the state’s transgender residents.

In July, Kelver protested Wyoming’s bathroom ban by using a women’s restroom in the State Capitol. She was neither charged nor sued for that direct action, despite a new law technically banning her from doing so.

September confrontation

Around 10 p.m. on the night of Saturday, September 13, a man identified in court documents as “S.D.” or “Durham,” and accompanied by at least two other men, shouted at Kelver from across Second Street, near the intersection with Ivinson Avenue.

There’s disagreement about what exactly he shouted.

“They had kind of looked over at us and yelled, ‘Fuck the Crowbar, that fag bar should close down,’” Kelver alleged during her interview with The Laramie Reporter.

The man’s own account of his outburst included no mention of slurs. The affidavit recounts what he allegedly told police:

“S.D. yelled ‘fuck the Crowbar’ because he likes the Buckhorn Bar better.”

Whatever the particulars, Kelver responded.

“I had stood up and just yelled, ‘Hey, what the fuck?’ And they had all turned their attention even more so towards me,” Kelver told the Reporter. “This was a while ago, so exactly what was yelled at me is kind of mixed up, but the first thing yelled, I remember specifically, was: ‘You heard me, you fucking faggot … And they continue yelling, you know: ‘What are you going to fucking do about it, faggot? Like, ‘I’ll fuck you up,’ and similar threats with … the slurs of ‘faggot’ and ‘tranny.’”

These alleged slurs are not included in the police report. In the affidavit, the man alleges he repeated only: “Fuck the Crowbar.”

Kelver approached the men, who also began crossing the street. Both parties met in the crosswalk.

The man identified as S.D. pushed Kelver to the ground, and Kelver responded by drawing a firearm from her satchel, a teal SCCY .380 subcompact pistol.

There was video footage of the altercation recorded by the ever-present Second and Ivinson downtown livestream, but it lacked audio. Laramie Police Officer Hunter Rope reviewed the footage, according to the affidavit, which recounts what the video showed:

At approximately [10:03 p.m.], Kelver appears to be in close proximity to S.D. with her hand on her right hip satchel, where the firearm was later located. [One second later], Kelver is pushed backwards by S.D. and takes a couple of steps back and falls to the ground, still with her hand to her right side … Kelver’s right hand removes what appears to be a firearm from the satchel on her right hip, grabs the firearm slide, and pulls it backwards, typically in a fashion to load a round into the chamber … Kelver, after pulling the firearm slide back, points the firearm to her left, where S.D. is present on camera, holding the firearm in her right hand.

“I’d hit the ground and I saw the three of them over me, and that is when I had reached into my side satchel,” she said. “The safety was on, and my finger was never on the trigger. I had simply wanted them to hear the chambering to get them away from me, and I had pointed the firearm in their direction — specifically at the individual who assaulted me.”

According to the affidavit and Kelver’s account, the men walked away after that. Kelver got off the ground, returned her gun to the satchel and went back to Crowbar’s side of the street
