# Fox 26 Houston 
A Louisiana grand jury in June declined to indict Christopher Gillum on a charge that could have carried up to a 15-year sentence, court records show.

Gillum, 45, of Chapel Hill, North Carolina, was freed this summer following the Louisiana grand jury's decision in June. He had been held without bond after authorities alleged he was preparing to commit an act of mass violence in New Orleans.

Gillum is not currently facing criminal charges in Louisiana or elsewhere, according to his attorney, Dylan Utley.

Katherine Schweit, a former Chicago prosecutor and FBI executive who ran the agency’s active shooter program, said that thwarted acts of mass violence do not always lead to indictments, but prosecutors often seek an array of criminal charges when possible.

"It’s very difficult to convince a jury, or to convince a grand jury, that something might have happened," Schweit told The Associated Press.

Lester Duhe, a spokesperson for Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill, said the alleged threat was communicated to a family member while Gillum was in North Carolina. Gillum also had not entered Louisiana before authorities arrested him in Florida.

Duhe said investigators additionally found that Gillum had been considering a trip to the Florida Keys during roughly the same period authorities believed he might have been traveling toward Louisiana. 

After his arrest, Gillum was extradited to Louisiana and accused of violating the state's law against "terrorizing." The statute applies in circumstances involving an imminent or ongoing violent crime or a situation that poses an impending danger to human life.
