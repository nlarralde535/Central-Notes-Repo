## The Latin Times

In conversations with senior aides, Trump has emphasized that the administration should prioritize arresting "bad guys" and reduce the visibility of large-scale operations that have drawn public scrutiny. "We've got to focus on the criminals," he has told advisers, according to one senior official cited by WSJ.

The shift comes as polling shows growing unease with the administration's approach, with a Washington Post/ABC News survey published in February finding 58% of Americans believing deportations are "going too far."

The recalibration is shaping both policy and messaging. Arrests by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) have declined to roughly 1,200 per day from more than 1,500 during earlier high-profile operations, according to people familiar with the data. Officials have also paused large-scale raids in major cities, while exploring more targeted enforcement, including cooperation with local jails and smaller workplace operations.

At the same time, Republican leaders and White House officials have begun steering lawmakers away from the phrase "mass deportation." During a closed-door retreat, Deputy Chief of Staff James Blair advised members of Congress to emphasize the removal of violent offenders instead, citing stronger public support for that approach, as Axios first reported.
