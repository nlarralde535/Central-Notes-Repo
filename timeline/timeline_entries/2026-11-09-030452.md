# News Center Maine

Records obtained by NEWS CENTER Maine showed that Homeland Security sent the jail a “Notice of Inspection” in January 2026 and in July flagged three former employees for invalid immigration paperwork. None of the four officers ICE has arrested in 2026 were flagged in HSI’s inspection.



On January 21, 2026, ICE agents arrested Emanuel Landila, a corrections officer recruit at the Cumberland County Jail. The next day, January 22, Cumberland County Sheriff Kevin Joyce held a press conference, slamming ICE for arresting Landila.

Sheriff Joyce called the agents’ tactics “bush league” and vouched for Landila, saying Landila was proactive in his immigration case.

Joyce said Landila left the Maine Criminal Justice Academy for three days, unannounced, to go to Texas for a court hearing about his immigration paperwork. Joyce said that while the unannounced departure was not part of Cumberland County Jail staff protocol, he said he understood Landila’s reasoning.

“This is an individual that had permission to be working in the state of Maine. We vetted him. He took the extra step of risking his job to fly to Texas to take care of hearings. And for some reason, he was picked up last night,” Joyce said in the January 22 press conference.

In an interview in October 2026, Joyce said, "He's not a criminal. He just went to court in Texas. I mean, what else can we expect from these folks?”



The next day, January 23, Homeland Security Investigations served the jail a “Notice of Inspection.” In the notice and a subpoena served with it, HSI demanded employment eligibility forms, payroll records, and hire dates for everyone who had worked at the jail over the past year.

A week later, January 30, Cumberland County’s lawyer responded with roughly 175 records.

In February, a Homeland Security spokesperson told media outlets, “It is only natural that we need to see that the sheriff isn’t employing any other lawbreakers.”

While Homeland Security reviewed those documents, ICE agents arrested another Cumberland County corrections officer.



County officials told NEWS CENTER Maine that Homeland Security's inspection did not flag any of the four CCJ corrections officers ICE arrested in 2026.

“They haven't done anything illegal that we can find in a polygraph,” Sheriff Joyce said. “Absent those three names, there were no other concerning names on the list that were provided. It begs the question, why were they arrested?"

ICE did not respond to NEWS CENTER Maine’s questions about the arrests.

Joyce said the county often consults immigration attorneys before hiring immigrants to see if their immigration status allows them to be employed.
