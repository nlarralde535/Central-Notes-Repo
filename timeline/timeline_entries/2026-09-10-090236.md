# WJLA ABC 7 Washington DC
The D.C. Police Union has accused Metropolitan Police Department (MPD) management of improperly using data from the Flock license-plate reader system and failing to establish safeguards for the powerful surveillance tool.

Union President Gregg Pemberton said the technology can be a legitimate investigative tool when used lawfully, but argued MPD has not negotiated, established or enforced adequate controls.

7News reached out to the police department. An MPD spokesperson said the Union's grievance is about police using a Flock camera in an internal affairs misconduct investigation.

"Department’s position is that the use of LPR in the misconduct investigation was appropriate. This grievance involves a union matter, and it will be resolved through arbitration between MPD and the DC Police Union," a spokesperson with MPD said. 

The union said it identified in July what it described as improper management use of Flock data. It filed misconduct complaints and a class grievance seeking to stop the alleged violations, according to the union.

The department also pointed out their License Plate Reader (LPR) policy was updated in April of 2026.

MPD denied the grievance and declined to investigate the managers accused of misusing the system, the union said. The department then issued a teletype restricting department-wide access to the system, according to the release.

7News also asked MPD what the nature of the misconduct investigation is and we are waiting to hear back.
