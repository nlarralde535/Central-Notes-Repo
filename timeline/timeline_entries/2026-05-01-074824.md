# San Antonio Express-News
While waiting for the school bus on Monday morning, two Alamo Heights Independent School District elementary schoolers and their step-mother were detained by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement as their classmates watched.

Fifth-grade student Victor Uzategui-Labrador and his younger sister, Victoria, who is in second grade, go to school at Cambridge Elementary, located in the heart of the small San Antonio enclave. The two children and their step-mom were transported to the ICE Immigration Processing Center in Dilley, Texas by 8 a.m. Tuesday. As of Friday afternoon, they were still detained there in the federal center. 

Originally from Venezuela, the Uzategui-Labrador family arrived in 2021 at the Southern U.S. border seeking asylum and presented themselves to U.S. Customs and Border Protection, according to the office of U.S. Rep. Joaquin Castro, who has advocated on behalf of the family since their detention. The parents — Victor Labrador and his wife, Maria Betania Uzategui Castillo — had valid work authorization until 2030, Castro’s office said. The family has enrolled its kids in Alamo Heights ISD since December 2021.

The Uzategui-Labrador family was originally scheduled for an immigration hearing in November 2027 but had another hearing scheduled for next Tuesday at 9 a.m.

The San Antonio family’s detention comes as a federal appeals court in late April rejected ICE’s mandatory detention policy, declaring it’s a flawed and constitutionally concerning interpretation of the law. 

A Department of Homeland Security spokesperson said the agency plans to take the case to the Supreme Court, according to Politico.
